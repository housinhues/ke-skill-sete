# Clause Library

This is the source of truth for contract language across all nine engagement types. When drafting, pull from here rather than writing clauses fresh — consistency is the point. Each clause has a plain-English purpose note, the actual wording pattern, and drafting rules pulled from hard-won lessons about where these contracts tend to go wrong.

**Legal disclaimer to carry into every drafting session**: this library gives Housing Hues consistent, sensible starting language — it is not a substitute for legal review. The non-negotiable clauses below should be reviewed by a South African attorney at least once before heavy production use, and again if the business model changes materially (e.g. expanding into international deals).

---

## Non-negotiable clauses

These appear in every contract, regardless of engagement type. Do not let a user or counterparty talk the skill into dropping one — if a user asks, explain why it's there and suggest they raise the *wording* with a lawyer rather than cutting it outright.

### Governing law & dispute resolution
**Rule: never leave this vague.** Every contract — even an informal-feeling collab between two talents — names an explicit governing law and dispute forum. Default: Republic of South Africa, with disputes resolved by binding arbitration under AFSA (Arbitration Foundation of Southern Africa) rules, seated in South Africa. Arbitration is the default over litigation because it stays private (talent and brand disputes don't need to become public court filings), it's faster and cheaper, and it's the standard choice for a talent agency operating at this scale. "The parties will resolve disputes amicably" is not a clause; it's a hope. Always pair it with a fallback mechanism. If a deal is ever large enough that litigation makes more sense, that's a per-contract override — not a reason to rewrite the default.

### Confidentiality
Standard mutual NDA-style language: neither party discloses the other's business terms, unreleased content, or internal communications without consent, surviving contract termination for a defined period (default: 2 years).

### IP / content ownership defaults
**Rule: separate ownership from usage rights — never conflate them (see Usage Rights below).** Default position: the creator/producer of content (photographer, videographer, 018 Productions, or other vendor) retains copyright unless explicitly assigned; the commissioning party (Housing Hues, talent, or brand) receives a license as defined in the Usage Rights clause. Full ownership transfer is a negotiable upgrade, not a default.

**For vendor/media partner contracts specifically**: state separately who owns (a) raw/unedited footage and (b) final delivered/edited footage — these are commonly different answers (e.g. vendor keeps raw footage rights, client owns final cut) and both need their own line, not one blended assumption.

### Brand-safety conduct (minimum standard)
Applies to every talent regardless of which social media tier (Managed/Independent) they're on: no content that disparages Housing Hues, breaches an active brand exclusivity clause, or violates the sponsored-content disclosure rule below. This is the floor, not the ceiling — it applies even to talent on the Independent tier.

### Sponsored-content disclosure
Not actually optional — most jurisdictions (South Africa's ASA code included) require clear disclosure of paid/sponsored content. State plainly: talent must disclose paid partnerships per applicable advertising standards. Do not frame this as a negotiable term even though it lives next to negotiable ones contextually.

### Termination for cause
Either party may terminate immediately for material breach, defined as: non-payment past 14 days from the invoice due date, conduct breaching brand-safety minimums, or illegal activity. The 14-day threshold is deliberately tight, in keeping with the house tone — decisive, not lenient, no drawn-out grace period. Keep it distinct from any earlier interest-accrual trigger set in the Fee & Payment clause below (e.g. interest starting day 7) — non-payment as *grounds to walk away* and non-payment as *the point it starts costing money* don't need to land on the same day. Distinguish clearly from the negotiable Cancellation/Kill-Fee clause below, which covers termination *without* cause.

### Document versioning
**Rule: every generated contract carries a version number and date in the footer** (e.g. "v1 — 15 Aug 2026"), enforced by the branding spec, not left to memory. When a contract is revised, increment the version rather than silently replacing the file, so superseded versions stay distinguishable at a glance.

## Negotiable clauses

These vary per deal. The skill fills these based on interview answers — the *options* below are what it draws from, not a fixed default to auto-apply.

### Fee & payment schedule
**Rule: this is the single highest-friction clause — draft it hardest.** Always state: exact amount, currency, payment trigger (on signing / on delivery / on invoice / milestone-based), and an explicit late-payment consequence (e.g. interest accrual after X days, or right to withhold further deliverables). Never leave payment terms as "to be agreed" in a final contract — if terms aren't settled, the contract isn't ready to send.

### Usage rights (license)
**Rule: always separate from ownership, and always time-bound.** Specify: which platforms/media, which territory, and — critically — an expiry date or duration. "Brand may use content" with no end date is a standing liability; even generous terms (e.g. "3 years from delivery") are far safer than open-ended.

### Exclusivity scope
Defines whether the talent is barred from similar deals with competing brands during (and optionally after) the engagement. Scope narrowly by category/competitor, not broadly by "any brand," unless the fee reflects a broad exclusivity.

### Social media posting — Managed vs Independent tier
**Rule: this must be an explicit, signed, dated choice per talent — never a silent default.**
- **Managed tier**: Housing Hues has approval rights over public-facing brand-relevant posts.
- **Independent tier**: talent retains full posting control, subject to the brand-safety conduct minimum (above).
Both tiers sit inside the same non-negotiable brand-safety floor — the choice is about *approval workflow*, not about whether conduct standards apply at all.

### Account access / credentials (social media management contracts)
Who holds login credentials during the engagement, how access is revoked at contract end, and — important — what happens to the account's content and following if the relationship ends (does the account revert fully to the talent/client, and on what timeline).

### Renewal / extension terms
Auto-renewal, opt-in renewal, or fixed-term-only. Default to opt-in (neither party is silently re-committed) unless the user specifies otherwise.

### Cancellation / kill-fee terms
Covers termination *without* cause — e.g. a brand pulling a campaign after signing but before delivery. Define a kill-fee scale (e.g. % of total fee based on how much work was already completed) rather than an all-or-nothing outcome.

## Drive folder & sharing note

Not a contract clause, but a related operational rule worth keeping next to this library: **don't auto-share a talent's Drive folder at creation.** Create it, but only share access with the talent after the contract is signed — an unsigned talent with edit access to their own "signed contracts" folder is an avoidable mess.
